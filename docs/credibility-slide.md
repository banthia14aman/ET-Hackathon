### Quote 1 — the decision-maker (the anchor quote)
> "What happens if Hormuz remains closed for another 30 days? We all have domestic situations, we have stocks… my comfort point used to be 60-60-60 — which means 60 days of crude oil, natural gas and LPG."
> — **Hardeep Singh Puri, Union Minister for Petroleum & Natural Gas**, 2026
> Source: [india.com report on the minister's statement](https://www.india.com/news/india/76-days-of-fuel-no-immediate-risk-oil-minister-hardeep-puri-makes-big-statement-amid-global-energy-woes-8440452/); see also his [Parliament statement on supply disruption measures (PIB)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2239021&reg=3&lang=1)

**Why this is the anchor:** India's petroleum minister, mid-crisis, reasons out loud in *days-of-cover* — the exact metric that ticks on TRINETRA's screen. Speaker line for the pitch: *"The minister's dashboard during the crisis was a number in his head — 60-60-60. TRINETRA puts that number on a wall, per refinery, per grade, recomputed every time the world changes."*

### Quote 2 — the analyst (the trade-off we make auditable)
> "The structural paradox India faces is that its most cost-efficient crude supply sources and its highest geopolitical-risk corridors are the same geography. Diversification makes India more resilient but also more expensive to supply."
> — Analysis of the Hormuz crisis, [Atlantic Council EnergySource](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/energysource/indias-energy-security-at-a-crossroads-the-hormuz-crisis-and-an-opportunity-for-us-india-cooperation/) / [India Narrative](https://www.indianarrative.com/opinion/the-strait-that-didnt-break-india-how-a-decade-of-preparation-defied-the-hormuz-crisis/)

**Why it's here:** cost-vs-resilience is exactly the trade-off the Decision Charter makes explicit and auditable (Article 2: when the security floor binds, the trace shows the money left on the table). Speaker line: *"That paradox is unresolvable by instinct in a war room. It's resolvable by a rule you wrote in advance — and we show you its price every time it fires."*

### Quote 3 — the market's own validation (Option C bridge)

## OPTION C — the market-as-validator framing (verbatim lines)

**Deck line (on the backtest slide, under the scorecard):**
`We didn't ask experts whether the plan is right. India's trading desks validated it — they executed the same pivot, weeks after our system would have surfaced it.`

**Verbal delivery (attach to Slide 5, ~15 seconds):**
> "One more thing about validation. We could have asked an expert to bless this. We did something harder: we let the market grade it. Every reroute in our top five — West Africa, the Americas, on-water Russian barrels — is a decision India's desks actually made in March and April, at spot premiums, under pressure. Our system surfaces the same option set in four minutes. The validator isn't a quote on a slide. It's the trade data."

**Q&A guard (if a judge asks "did any practitioner review this?"):**
> "No — deliberately, for a one-week build. We used two validators that can't be flattered: public record and market behavior. The minister's own crisis metric — days of cover — is our core display. And the option set was graded against what desks actually executed, with the misses reported. A practitioner advisor is week two; we'd rather show you an honest scorecard than a borrowed endorsement."
